The line is not a clock

Ten worlds, ninety chapters, thirty-six sources. The timelines are an editorial arrangement — and that arrangement can be measured. This is the dataset behind the site, and what it reveals about the way we draw deep time.

  • 90 chapters
  • 10 worlds
  • 36 sources
  • Snapshot 2026-10-27

Introduction

The timelines on this site are drawn as lines. The space between chapters is chosen to keep the recent record legible, not to measure elapsed time. That is a defensible editorial choice. It is also a transformation of the data — and transformations should be disclosed.

This page publishes the table behind the timelines, measures what the layout does to time, and states what the sample cannot support. Every number below is generated from data/events.csv by analysis/audit.py; none of it is typed into the charts by hand.

02 The sample

A sample of ninety moments

Every world has nine chapters. That is a format constraint, not a finding. The marks below sit at their editorial positions, so the pattern is a property of the design as much as of the history.

The 46 deep-time chapters describe formation and geology. The 34 dated chapters describe telescopes, missions, and measurements. The 10 Today states close each line. The mix is the sample.

39% of Jupiter's line before its first dated chapter
74% of Mars's line before its first dated chapter
0 dated chapters on Earth's line; its record is deep time plus Today

The dated record does not begin at the same place on every line. It starts late and clusters at the recent end. That is a property of what the sample chose to observe, not a claim that nothing was recorded earlier.

03 The distortion

A 44× stretch at the recent end

The top bar is the line as published. The bottom bar is the same eight intervals drawn at true duration. The last interval — Chicxulub to today — receives 21% of the line while representing 0.48% of Earth's 13.8 billion years.

The first interval — Big Bang to Protoplanet — receives 7.5% of the line and represents 66.67% of the time. The line buys legibility at the recent end by spending it at the far end.

43.9× expansion of the final interval relative to a linear axis
399× the final interval's stretch divided by the first's
0.48% of Earth's time in the last 21% of its line

A linear axis would be honest about duration and unreadable about people. The editorial axis makes the recent record visible — as long as the trade-off is named.

The final interval is 66 million years long because the preceding chapter is Chicxulub. It includes the whole of human history, which is invisible at the scale of a 13.8-billion-year line. The distortion is the mechanism that makes that history drawable at all.

04 The silences

What the line leaves out

A gap is the distance between consecutive chapters. It is a property of the sample, not evidence that nothing happened. The bars below normalize each world's largest gap by that world's own span, so a longer history does not automatically produce a larger share.

93% of Saturn's span is covered by its largest gap
9.2 billion years the longest gap in years, on Earth's line
400 million years median gap between consecutive deep-time chapters

Earth's gap is the longest in absolute years: 9.2 billion years between the Big Bang and the protoplanet. Saturn's is the largest share of its own span. The two measures answer different questions, and the chart deliberately uses the second.

05 The record

Two tempos, one table

The sample contains two regimes. Deep-time chapters are separated by hundreds of millions of years. Dated observations are separated by decades or years. A single average would describe neither.

400 million years median deep-time gap
vs
55 years median dated-record gap

Each median compares like with like: only deep-to-deep and dated-to-dated gaps enter the two figures. The 19 boundary gaps — wherever deep time meets the dated record or Today — are excluded from both, so neither median borrows the other regime's tempo.

71% of the dated chapters fall in just two centuries — the 1600s and 1900s. The dated record is a history of observation, not a uniform sample of time. Only 9 of the ten worlds have a dated chapter at all.

07 Methods & limits

What this can and cannot say

This is a curated comparative sample, not a random sample, a complete chronology, or a time series. The analysis is designed to make that limitation visible rather than to hide it behind a single score.

The sample

Ten worlds, nine chapters each. The order and start label for each world live in worlds.csv. The equal chapter count is a format constraint, not a finding.

The numbers

Ages are years before 2026. layout_at is an editorial coordinate from 0 to 1 and must never be used to estimate elapsed time. A blank range means no numeric range was assigned, not certainty.

The pipeline

analysis/audit.py validates the three CSVs, derives the charts, and writes analysis.json plus the audit report. --check fails if either artifact is stale.

Limitations

  • The ninety chapters are a curated sample, chosen to tell a coherent story. They are not a random or complete inventory of events.
  • The largest gap is a property of the sample. It is not evidence that nothing happened during that interval.
  • “Today” is a snapshot at 2026-10-27, not a live feed. Planned missions are marked as planned.
  • Age ranges are recorded only where cited sources genuinely disagree. Most rows have a point age.
  • The globes are illustrations. Only Earth uses spacecraft imagery; the other surfaces are procedural and not spatially registered.
  • The dated record is a history of observation. Its clusters reflect telescopes, spacecraft, and access to instruments, not the underlying rate of events.
